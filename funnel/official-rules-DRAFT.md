> **Structure note for counsel (changed Nov 23, 2026):** until Nov 23, 2026 this draft described a pure judged contest of skill. It is no longer that. The promotion is now a **hybrid** with three tiers:
> 1. **Grand prize:** judged. One winner chosen by Sponsor's panel against the published criteria in Section 6. Skill and fit, not chance.
> 2. **Second prize:** awarded by **random drawing** to [N] entrants. This is a chance-based tier.
> 3. **Third prize:** awarded to **multiple entrants**. How they are chosen and how many is undecided [DECIDE: how third-prize winners are chosen and how many].
>
> **Why this matters.** A judged contest avoids the lottery concerns (prize, chance, consideration) because chance is not an element. A random drawing for a prize is the "chance" element, so the second-prize tier is a sweepstakes-type game of chance. Any requirement that looks like consideration (for example a required interview) can turn the drawing into an illegal lottery. State registration, bonding, odds-disclosure and rule-change requirements for games of chance now apply to the second-prize tier. The earlier draft's position, that registration and bonding rules for games of chance do not apply because the whole promotion is skill-based, no longer holds for the second prize. The judged grand prize still depends on Sponsor scoring every judged applicant against Section 6 as published and keeping the scorecards.
