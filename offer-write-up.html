01

What the video is

Paul Fontinelli runs Boulder Adventure Park, a $22M indoor family entertainment center in Grand Prairie, Texas. He is doing $4.8M a year, needs $550K a month to break even, is at $400K, and carries $180K a month in debt service. He has 14 days of cash and is about to put his entire 401(k) in. Hormozi has one session to hand him a play that generates cash in 90 days.

Hormozi's diagnosis in one line: Paul tracks outputs (revenue, admissions) and not inputs (leads, cost per lead, show rate, close rate). The park is demand constrained at 50% of peak capacity, so the fix is a demand play, not an operations play.

The one bet

"You don't have the luxury of time, so we make one bet." He skipped the season pass and went all-in on summer camp, because that is the product parents were already deciding on right then.

02

The offer, piece by piece

Anchorvalue
$500/week × 10 weeks = $5,000 "value"

Everything is priced against the full retail stack so the real ticket ($2,000 for the whole summer) feels like a discount. "Buy 4 weeks, get 6 free" and "buy one month, get the whole summer" are the same price said three ways. One of them clicks.

Reasonwhy
Early bird

Every offer needs a stated reason it exists, or people assume it is always available. Early bird is the reason here; in roofing it is the storm, the season, the insurance deadline.

Hookfront end
The giveaway

First prize: a $5,000 private full-summer camp, free. Second prize: $3,000 off (which is the core $2,000 offer). Multiple second-place winners, legally disclosed. Lead with the biggest free thing; it anchors the price for the phone call later so nobody has to be "sold up."

7 dayscountdown
Launch window with timer

Countdown timer at the top of the page, daily email and text with days left. A third to a half of all leads arrive in the last 48 hours; the middle feels dead and you keep spending anyway.

PageVSL
Landing page rules

One banner, not four. Headline, video, button. Trust sits next to the action (parent rating beside the button). Answer the real fear with a hard stat ("zero falls last year"). Day-one money back. The VSL became the best converting ad they ever ran.

Thank-youviral loop
Referral entries

Copyable link on the thank-you page. Every friend who opts in is one more entry. If the person you referred wins, you win too. Parents want their kids to go with friends; homeowners want the neighbor to use the same roofer.

72 hrsclaim
Second-prize claim window

After the drawing, everyone else "won" second prize with a 72-hour claim timer. No deadline, no claim.

Phonesbottleneck
Call every lead, sell the core offer

The giveaway produces the list; the sales team produces the cash. Hormozi flagged the three-person sales team as the risk before they even launched.

Afterevergreen
Flip to the evergreen "buy X get Y" campaign

Run it "until the cows come home," then re-run the whole giveaway on the next product (birthdays), year-round, at a tenth of the spend.

What happened in 90 days

  • 3,700 leads in a local market, about 120 a day.
  • Summer camp revenue up 325% over the prior year.
  • Cheapest cost per lead the business had ever seen.
  • Admissions doubled in 4 to 6 weeks once licensed creator video ran as ads.

What capped it

  • "We didn't have the manpower to close the leads." No SDR layer, no call sequence.
  • Hormozi's fix: hire salespeople as growth spend. With lead flow they always out-earn their cost.
  • This gap is the whole 2URN opportunity. The play is public. Executing it inside a contractor's business is not.
03

Why it maps to roofing almost perfectly

Hormozi says businesses behave in patterns. A roofer and a theme park share the same economic shape: high-ticket, local, seasonal, demand constrained, owner tracks revenue not inputs, sales capacity is the real ceiling. The differences work in roofing's favor.

Compliance to build in, not bolt on
  • Sweepstakes law. No purchase necessary, official rules, alternate method of entry, state registration above thresholds (New York and Florida register at $5,000). Hormozi said it twice; we write it into the SOP once.
  • Insurance restoration. Never advertise a waived or paid deductible. Second prize must be a discount on retail or a free upgrade, framed so it does not touch the insured amount. Texas and many states treat deductible-eating as a crime.
  • Referral loop. Extra entries for referrals are fine in most states. Check the ones that treat it as consideration.
04

Layer one: the play we install inside the roofer

This is the product. 2URN comes in as the fractional CMO and leaves behind a lead engine the roofer's own people run. The giveaway is the launch; the evergreen offer, the setter seat and the dashboard are what stays.

Days 1–14install
Inputs, offer, page, setter

Audit the inputs Hormozi asked Paul for: leads, cost per lead, set rate, show rate, close rate, average ticket. Build the core evergreen offer and the "buy X get Y" variants. One-banner landing page with owner VSL, trust beside the button, warranty stat. Hire or assign one setter; install the call sequence (speed-to-lead under 5 minutes, 7 touches in 72 hours).

Days 15–24launch
Free Roof Giveaway, 7-day countdown

First prize: full replacement. Second prize: $2,500 off or a named upgrade, multiple winners. Giveaway ads built from the owner's own footage and the 2URN hook library. Daily SMS and email countdown. Referral link on the thank-you page. Model the 48-hour spike so the owner does not panic on day 4.

Days 25–30convert
Drawing, 72-hour claim, phones

Announce the winner publicly (this is the content and the PR). Everyone else gets second prize with a 72-hour claim window. Setter calls every lead to book a free inspection. Owner or closer runs the in-home visit with the anchored price already in the homeowner's head.

Days 31–90evergreen
Flip to the core campaign, scale the seat

Run the evergreen offer on paid. Reinvest campaign cash into ad spend. Add a second setter when the first one is at capacity. Weekly input scorecard with the owner. Prep the next seasonal giveaway (storm, pre-winter, spring).

What they own at day 90

  • Evergreen offer and a giveaway playbook they can re-run
  • Landing page and VSL, in their GHL account
  • A trained setter and a written call sequence
  • An input dashboard (leads, CPL, set, show, close, ticket)
  • Ad account with pixel, audiences and a winning creative library

What 2URN already has for this

  • Roofer landing-page builder and survey-funnel skill (GHL)
  • Creative engine: 5 campaigns, hooks, call-out standard
  • Proposal sites and the sales-call-to-proposal pipeline
  • Platform data on which zones and offers convert (HSH)
  • Hormozi's missing piece is the piece we are good at: the call sequence and the setter.

07

Objections the play already answers

"I don't want to give away a roof"

You are already discounting to win jobs. One roof at cost, announced publicly, buys a list of every homeowner in your metro thinking about a roof this season. The calculator shows it is paid back by the second sale.

"Giveaway leads are tire-kickers"

True if nobody calls them. The anchor and the second prize filter for people who want a roof; the setter and the 72-hour claim convert them. Paul's problem was not lead quality, it was manpower.

"Why not just buy leads from you?"

You can (HSH does that). This is for the roofer who wants the asset. Pay-per-lead rents demand; the install owns it. Offer both and let the price difference do the sorting.

"We tried Facebook, it didn't work"

Ask for the inputs. Almost every time it is no pixel, no offer, no follow-up and a budget that never left learning phase. Same thing we found on a recent ad account audit.
