## The problem we actually solve

The leads were never the problem. Nobody called them. In almost every home-services CRM we open, a lead arrives mid-afternoon, gets one call that night, one voicemail, and a dead tag. The homeowner filled out three forms and went with whoever picked up first.

That is the hidden lock. Agencies cannot fix it because they work outside the business: they send leads and the owner is on a roof, in an attic or at a measure. The problem lives in the ten feet between the CRM and the phone.

The master key is three things installed together, inside the business:

1. A setter seat with a written sequence: a call in under five minutes, seven touches in 72 hours, every outcome logged.
2. An offer and launch that fills the pipeline on day one, so the setter has volume to work and the owner sees the engine run.
3. An input scorecard the owner reads weekly: leads, cost per lead, set rate, show rate, close rate, average ticket. Revenue is the output; these are the inputs. Owners who only watch revenue cannot see which part is broken.

Alex Hormozi ran this exact launch on a $22M family entertainment park in 2026: 3,700 leads in one metro in 7 days, 325% growth in the product line. The one thing that capped it was manpower to call the leads. That gap is the whole reason this offer exists.

## Scorecard: the inputs we report weekly

Six numbers, every Monday, same order, with a target and a who. The owner learns to read inputs instead of revenue, and we can see which part of the engine is broken before the month closes.

| Input | Definition | Target (first 90 days) | Who moves it |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Leads | Form entries from the campaign, deduplicated | 500 in the launch month; 150+/month evergreen | Media buyer, creative |
| Cost per lead | Ad spend / leads | $15 or under | Media buyer, offer |
| Speed to lead | Median minutes from entry to first call attempt | Under 5 during business hours, under 60 after hours | Setter |
| Set rate | Appointments booked / leads contacted | 25% or higher | Setter, script |
| Show rate | Appointments held / booked | 65% or higher | Setter (confirmation sequence) |
| Close rate | Jobs sold / appointments held | 30% | Closer, offer, anchor |
| Average ticket | Revenue / jobs sold | At or above the roofing baseline from the audit | Closer, upgrade second prize |

Two outputs sit under the inputs: cost per sold job and gross profit after ads. They are reported but never discussed first.

Escalation: any input under target two weeks running gets a named fix on the owner call, and the fix is one of three things: creative, the sequence, or the person on the phone.
